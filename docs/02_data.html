Headline findings

  1. The file is clean: 14,610 rows, no gaps, no duplicates, no NaN, no negatives, one PET value of exactly 0.0 mm/d (1966-01-14, physically plausible mid-winter value, not a missing-data code). Q is strictly positive (min 0.069 mm/d) — the river is perennial, so log/Box-Cox transforms are safe without offsets.
  2. Units are mm/day and the basin area is ~1,950 km² (Section 2.2): the discrete quantum of the Q series (0.0012544 mm/d) is exactly 1 ft³/s spread over 1,950 km² (753 mi²), i.e. the series was converted from USGS daily-mean discharge in cfs. This matches USGS 02472000 Leaf River near Collins, MS (752 mi²).
  3. The record starts 1948-10-01 — confirmed independently from the PET/P/Q seasonal phase (Section 6.1). This verifies the resolution of ISSUE-001 (periods.definition: dates).
  4. PET is not a repeated climatology: same-day-of-year inter-annual SD is 1.30 mm/d against a seasonal amplitude of 1.83 mm/d — PET carries real daily weather information (Section 6).
  5. The catchment is strongly nonlinear: event runoff coefficients range 0.004–0.45 over 31 isolated events and correlate with antecedent flow (r = 0.57) and event depth (r = 0.60) (Section 8.4).
  6. Memory: recession constant k ≈ 0.90 d⁻¹ (9 d residence time), log-Q ACF e-folding 48 d, and a linear P-lag model keeps gaining skill up to ~1 year (R² 0.59 at 10 d → 0.63 at 90 d → 0.65 at 365 d) → ≥180-day input sequences for ML models, ≥365-day warm-up for conceptual models (Section 11).
  7. The evaluation period is wetter than calibration: eval-metric mean Q is 1.29× the calibration mean and WY1949 is the wettest year of the 40-year record (P rank 1/40) — a genuine extrapolation risk (Section 10).

def const_runs(v):
    g = np.r_[0, (np.diff(v) != 0).cumsum()]
    s = pd.Series(v).groupby(g)
    size, first = s.size(), s.first().to_numpy()
    return size, first

rows = []

def lyne_hollick(q, a=0.925, passes=3):
    b = q.copy()
    for p in range(passes):
        y = b[::-1] if p % 2 else b
        f = np.zeros_like(y); f[0] = y[0]
        for i in range(1, len(y)):
            f[i] = a*f[i-1] + (1+a)/2*(y[i]-y[i-1])
        bf = np.minimum(y, y - np.maximum(f, 0))
        b = bf[::-1] if p % 2 else bf
    return np.clip(b, 0, q)

def pettitt(x):
    n = len(x); r = np.argsort(np.argsort(x)) + 1
    U = np.array([2*r[:k].sum() - k*(n+1) for k in range(1, n+1)])

11 Implications for downstream agents

a03 (model proposer) — processes, memory, nonlinearity

  • Two storage time scales are mandatory: a fast one (event response peaks 1–3 d after rain, Richards–Baker 0.32) and a slow one (BFI 0.41, log-Q ACF e-folding 48 d, recession τ ≈ 9 d for the intermediate store and much longer tails).
  • Recession is non-linear (Brutsaert–Nieber b = 1.68 ≠ 1): prefer a non-linear lower zone (SAC-SMA/HBV-style) or ≥2 parallel linear reservoirs (HYMOD’s quick tanks + slow tank).
  • Runoff generation is strongly threshold-controlled: event runoff coefficients span 0.004–0.45 and scale with antecedent flow (r = 0.57) → a saturation-excess / variable-contributing-area formulation (Probability-Distributed soil Moisture, as in HYMOD, or SAC-SMA’s tension stores) is the natural choice.
  • Seasonality comes from PET, not P: mean P varies 2.6–5.1 mm/d over the year while mean Q varies 0.38–2.88 mm/d. Models must reproduce the summer evaporative shutdown (JJA: P 330 mm, PET 409 mm, Q 42 mm) — no snow processes are needed in south Mississippi.
  • Long memory: a linear model on the last L days of P keeps improving to L ≈ 365 (R² 0.59 → 0.65). Recommended LSTM sequence length 180–365 days (cf. Kratzert et al. 2018) (use 365 if memory allows, 180 as a cheap default); conceptual warm-up ≥ 365 days — which is what the calibration window already provides (1951-10-01…1952-09-30). Note the evaluation warm-up prescribed by the protocol is only 92 days (Oct–Dec 1948), so models should be initialised from a plausible near-median storage state rather than from empty stores.
  • PET is not climatological (same-DOY inter-annual SD 1.30 mm/d): feed the actual PET series, do not replace it with a seasonal curve.

a04 (metrics advisor) — distribution and objective transformation

  • Daily Q: skewness 6.8, kurtosis 77, Q5/Q95 = 47, log-normal-shaped in log space; the wettest 1 % of days carry 16 % and the wettest 5 % carry 40 % of the total flow volume. Untransformed squared errors (NSE/KGE on Q) are dominated by ~20 flood days per year.
  • Q is strictly positive (min 0.069 mm/d) → log, sqrt and Box-Cox transforms need no offset; sensible complements to the mandated KGE/NSE during development are KGE on log Q or sqrt Q, or a split KGE / FDC-signature diagnostics (Yilmaz et al. 2008). Final reporting stays KGE + NSE on untransformed Q, per config/project.yaml.
  • Calibration-period moments for the KGE β/α terms: mean 1.3533 mm/d, sd 2.8511 mm/d.

a05–a09 (model builders) — units and practicalities

  • All three series are mm/day; no unit conversion is needed anywhere. Basin area ≈ 1,950 km² if a discharge in m³/s is ever required (1 mm/d = 22.57 m³/s).
  • P has 54.9 % exact zeros; Q has no zeros and no NaN → no special missing-value or zero-flow handling.
  • Never derive scaling constants from anything but Section 10.1.

a10 (optimisation advisor) — response-surface expectations

  • 13,149 calibration days with a heavy-tailed target: the objective surface for untransformed NSE/KGE is dominated by a handful of flood years (WY1961, WY1974, WY1980) and is therefore rough; global search (SCE-UA/DE) rather than local gradient descent is advised, consistent with the fact that this exact data set is the SCE-UA benchmark (Duan et al. 1992).
  • Strong parameter interaction is expected between the fast/slow partition (BFI ≈ 0.41) and the recession constants (k ≈ 0.90).

a16 (evaluator) — risks

  • The evaluation window is the wettest part of the record (Q mean ratio 1.29, WY1949 the wettest year). A model calibrated on 1952–1988 will most likely show a positive α and a slight negative β bias there; report the KGE decomposition, not just the total.
  • The evaluation warm-up is only 92 days — differences between models in initial-state handling will be visible in Jan–Mar 1949. Warm-up rows are not scored (metric starts 1949-01-01).

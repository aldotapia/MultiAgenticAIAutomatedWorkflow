1 Purpose and scope

This report selects and fully specifies the five models that agents a05–a09 will implement and a11–a15 will calibrate. It is written so that a builder can code its slot from config/models.yaml plus the corresponding section here without reading any other document.

Three requirements drive every choice:

  1. Maximise the chance of the best possible reproduction of daily \(Q\). Independent benchmarks at this exact gauge (USGS 02472000) put calibrated conceptual models at NSE 0.69–0.82 (SAC-SMA 0.796, HBV 0.819, FUSE 0.775–0.779, VIC 0.72, mHM 0.70) and regional LSTMs at NSE 0.84–0.86 (Kratzert et al. 2019). Our target band is therefore NSE 0.80–0.87 on calibration and 0.74–0.85 on evaluation.
  2. Span a complexity gradient so that the study can answer does complexity pay off?
  3. Be complementary, because a16 also evaluates the ensembles pre-declared in config/models.yaml → ensembles: (Section 12).

Everything below is constrained by CLAUDE.md rule 3: no statistic of observed \(Q\) inside 1948-10-01…1951-09-30 may influence any model choice, feature, bound or scaling constant. All numbers quoted from data in this report come from the calibration window only.

3.3 Why these five, and what was rejected

Selected: ARX–API ridge (slot 1), GR4J (2), HBV-light without snow (3), SAC-SMA without Snow-17 (4), single-basin LSTM (5).

The screening is not a pure ranking exercise: slot 1 is included by design even though HYMOD scores marginally higher (3.90 vs 3.84). A statistical lower benchmark is mandatory for the project’s central question (“does complexity pay off?”) — without it the gradient has no origin — and its low process score is precisely the point. Everything above slot 1 in the screening that is not selected is rejected for the reasons below.

Rejections and overrides, with reasons:

  • HYMOD is not used, although it is the historical Leaf River model (Boyle et al. 2000; Vrugt et al. 2003). The skill’s default set proposes it; I override it and log the decision. HYMOD routes its slow response through a single linear reservoir, which is exactly the structure a02 falsifies (\(b = 1.68 \ne 1\), and 9-d vs 549-d time scales). Its production store is a Moore PDM (Moore 1985), i.e. conceptually the same variable-contributing-area idea already carried by GR4J’s production store and HBV’s \(\beta\) function, so it would add little complementarity at 5 parameters — a slot almost identical in complexity to GR4J’s 4. Replacing it with HBV-light buys (i) an explicit threshold UZL in the fast response, (ii) two subsurface time scales, (iii) the best documented at-gauge conceptual score (NSE 0.819, a01), and (iv) it fills the complexity gap between 4 and 15 parameters. The historical Leaf River lineage is preserved through SAC-SMA, which is the model of Duan et al. (1992), Sorooshian et al. (1993) and Boyle et al. (2000) on this very record.
  • TOPMODEL requires a topographic-index distribution; a01 only provides a single GAGES-II TWI value (12.69), so the index histogram would have to be invented. Rejected on data demands.
  • Xinanjiang / PDM / FUSE / MARRMoT score well on process but add nothing that SAC-SMA + HBV do not already cover, and FUSE/MARRMoT would mean porting a framework (high implementation risk in one session).
  • Gradient boosting / GRU / TCN: a tree ensemble cannot carry state, so it needs the same hand-made antecedent features as slot 1 while losing the closed-form transparency; GRU/TCN are near-duplicates of the LSTM with less hydrological literature. Rejected on complementarity.
  • dPL / differentiable hybrid (Feng et al. 2022) scores highest on process and complementarity but has the worst implementation risk (a torch time-stepping loop with smoothed thresholds, trained jointly with a network, written blind by one agent in one session) and the worst compute score. The hybrid idea is not lost: it re-enters as the pre-declared ensembles ens_physics_plus_ml and ens_best_conceptual_plus_lstm (Section 12), which a16 evaluates with weights fixed on calibration data only — the same scientific question at a fraction of the risk.

5 Slot 1 — ARX–API ridge regression

5.1 Overview

A persistence-free linear model: the predictors are non-linear functions of the forcing only (\(P\), \(PET\), day of year); no observed or simulated \(Q\) enters the right-hand side, so the comparison with the conceptual slots is fair (a catchment model also only sees \(P\) and \(PET\)). The two ideas that lift it above a naive regression are (i) antecedent precipitation indices (API), the oldest storage proxy in hydrology and the backbone of IHACRES-type models (Jakeman and Hornberger 1993), and (ii) a Box–Cox target transform (Box and Cox 1964) with a bias-corrected back-transform (Duan 1983), which handles the extreme skew of daily \(Q\) (a02: skew 6.85). Estimation is ridge regression (Hoerl and Kennard 1970), i.e. closed form, so one model evaluation costs one \(\sim 22 \times 22\) linear solve.

Why for the Leaf River. a02 shows that the \(R^2\) of a linear model on lagged \(P\) rises monotonically with the lag window (0.59 at 10 d → 0.65 at 365 d): most of the daily signal is input memory. This slot quantifies exactly how much, and thereby prices the conceptual structure of slots 2–4.

5.3 Perceptual → conceptual assumptions

Represented: catchment wetness memory at three time scales; a variable runoff coefficient through the \(P_t \times A_t\) interaction terms; the drying effect of accumulated PET; residual seasonality. Ignored: mass conservation (nothing forces \(\sum \hat Q \le \sum P\) except the bias factor), explicit states, threshold behaviour beyond the quadratic/interaction terms, and any non-stationarity.

5.4 Mathematical formulation

Recursive feature states, computed forward over the contiguous forcing frame with zero initial values:

\[A^{(j)}_t = \lambda_j\,A^{(j)}_{t-1} + P_t,\qquad j \in \{\text{fast},\text{med},\text{slow}\},\qquad A^{(j)}_{-1}=0 \tag{1}\]

\[E^{\text{ant}}_t = \lambda_e\,E^{\text{ant}}_{t-1} + \mathrm{PET}_t,\qquad E^{\text{ant}}_{-1}=0 \tag{2}\]

The effective memory of Equation 1 is \(1/(1-\lambda_j)\) days, hence the bounds in Table 4 (1.4–5 d, 5–20 d, 20–1000 d), which bracket the a02 response lag (1–3 d), recession time (9.1 d) and the GAGES-II contact time (549 d).

Design row (with $n_= $ n_lag, \(d_t\) = day of year, \(\omega = 2\pi/365.25\)):

\[ \mathbf{z}_t = \Big[\,1,\; P_t,\dots,P_{t-n_\ell},\; A^{(f)}_t, A^{(m)}_t, A^{(s)}_t,\; E^{\text{ant}}_t,\; \sqrt{P_t},\; \tfrac{P_t A^{(m)}_t}{100},\; \tfrac{P_t A^{(s)}_t}{100},\; \big(\tfrac{A^{(s)}_t}{100}\big)^2,\; \sin\omega d_t,\cos\omega d_t,\sin 2\omega d_t,\cos 2\omega d_t \,\Big] \tag{3}\]

With \(n_\ell = 5\) that is 21 columns including the intercept. Target transform (Box–Cox):

\[ y_t = g(Q_t;\lambda_{bc}) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{Q_t^{\lambda_{bc}} - 1}{\lambda_{bc}}, & \lambda_{bc} > 10^{-6}\\[4pt] \ln Q_t, & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases} \qquad g^{-1}(y) = \begin{cases}\big(\max(\lambda_{bc} y + 1,\,10^{-9})\big)^{1/\lambda_{bc}}\\ e^{y}\end{cases} \tag{4}\]

(\(Q>0\) always, a02, so no offset is needed.) Columns 2… of \(Z\) are standardised with the fitting-window mean and standard deviation; the intercept column is left alone and is not penalised:

\[\hat{\boldsymbol\beta} = \big(Z^\top Z + \alpha\,n\,I'\big)^{-1} Z^\top \mathbf{y}, \qquad \alpha = 10^{\texttt{log10\_ridge\_alpha}},\quad I'=\mathrm{diag}(0,1,\dots,1) \tag{5}\]

Output equation, with the multiplicative bias correction \(c\) estimated on the fitting window only:

\[\hat Q_t = c\cdot\max\!\big(g^{-1}(\mathbf{z}_t^\top\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}),\,0.05\big), \qquad c = \frac{\sum_{t\in\text{fit}} Q_t}{\sum_{t\in\text{fit}}\max(g^{-1}(\mathbf{z}_t^\top\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}),0.05)} \tag{6}\]

5.6 Initial states and warm-up

\(A^{(j)}_{-1}=E^{\text{ant}}_{-1}=0\). With \(\lambda_{\text{slow}} \le 0.999\) the slowest recursion needs \(\approx 3/(1-\lambda) \approx 3000\) d to be fully spun up in the worst case, but \(\ge 365\) d removes >97 % of the transient for \(\lambda \le 0.992\); warmup_days_min: 365 is declared, and a11 must keep \(\lambda_{\text{slow}} \le 0.995\) if the evaluation warm-up (92 d) is a concern — the model is standardised, so a residual spin-up offset is largely absorbed by the intercept.

5.7 Mass balance check

Not applicable (no stores). The builder must instead assert, on the fitting window, \(0.9 \le \overline{\hat Q}/\overline{Q} \le 1.1\) (guaranteed by Equation 6 up to the clipping) and \(\hat Q_t > 0\;\forall t\).

5.8 Numerical pitfalls

  • Standardise the design columns before the ridge solve, otherwise \(\alpha\) means something different for every column; store the means/sds in the artefact and reuse them at prediction time.
  • Use scipy.linalg.solve(A, b, assume_a="pos"), never np.linalg.inv.
  • Clip \(\lambda_{bc}y+1\) at \(10^{-9}\) before the fractional power (Equation 4), otherwise NaNs appear for strongly negative predictions.
  • n_lag arrives from a continuous optimizer: round it (int(round(x))) and clip to [1, 10].
  • The first n_lag rows of the frame have undefined lags: fill with 0 (dry) and exclude them from fitting.

5.9 Implementation route

From scratch, numpy + scipy.linalg. kind: "trainable", so: fit(forcing, target, params) builds Equation 3, solves Equation 5 and stores \((\hat\beta, \text{col means/sds}, c, \lambda_{bc}, n_\ell)\); simulate(forcing, params) rebuilds the features on the frame it is given and applies the stored coefficients. Lazy-fit rule: if simulate is called on an unfitted instance it must fit itself on the calibration window obtained through src.common.data (never slice_run(..., "evaluation")) and emit a warning. save/load must round-trip a plain .npz/.json artefact.

Fitting protocol (also in config/models.yaml → fit_protocol): coefficients on calibration_internal.train, hyperparameters selected on calibration_internal.validation, then a final closed-form refit of the coefficients on the full calibration metric window with the selected hyperparameters. No evaluation data at any stage.

5.10 Expected behaviour

Strengths: unbiased in the mean by construction, extremely cheap, no equifinality drama, and a transparent statement of “how much is memory”. Weaknesses: no mass conservation, it will smear flood peaks (a linear map cannot produce a 45 mm/d spike from a 100 mm storm on dry soil), and recessions decay as a mixture of exponentials fixed by \(\lambda_j\) rather than by storage. Expected NSE 0.60–0.75 (calibration), 0.50–0.72 (evaluation); KGE \(\alpha\) clearly below 1 (variance under-dispersion).

6 Slot 2 — GR4J

6.1 Overview

GR4J (Perrin et al. 2003) is the four-parameter daily lumped model of the French “Génie Rural” family, developed by successive structural simplification tested on 429 catchments. It is the standard parsimony benchmark in comparative hydrology and is available as the R package airGR (Coron et al. 2017), which the builder should use as a numerical test oracle if R is available (it is not required). Wang and Gupta (2024) applied GR-type and other lumped structures to this very record.

Why for the Leaf River. GR4J satisfies four of the five hard requirements with four parameters: a soil-moisture-dependent production function (variable runoff coefficient), ET that can reach PET, a non-linear routing store with \(Q_r \propto R^5\) for small \(R\) — the direct analogue of the observed Brutsaert–Nieber \(b = 1.68\) — and a 1–3 d unit hydrograph. Its weak point here is that it has one slow store; the exchange term \(x_2\) then has to absorb the long-term balance, which makes it an interesting diagnostic (a01 flags inter-catchment exchange as a MEDIUM-priority process).

Represented: soil-moisture control of the runoff coefficient (saturation-excess in a bulk sense), ET limited by soil water, percolation to a routing store, non-linear release, translation delay, and a net gain/loss of groundwater across the topographic divide. 6.4 Mathematical formulation

State variables: \(S\) (production store, mm, \(0\le S\le x_1\)), \(R\) (routing store, mm, \(R\ge 0\)), plus the two unit-hydrograph memory vectors. Order of operations within day \(t\) (explicit, operator-split; this is the scheme actually used, not an ODE solver):

(1) Net forcing. With \(E = \mathrm{PET}_t\): \[P_n = \max(P_t - E,\,0),\qquad E_n = \max(E - P_t,\,0) \quad(\text{only one is non-zero}) \tag{7}\]

(2) Rain entering the production store. \[P_s = \frac{x_1\left(1-\left(\frac{S}{x_1}\right)^2\right)\tanh\!\frac{P_n}{x_1}} {1+\frac{S}{x_1}\tanh\!\frac{P_n}{x_1}} \tag{8}\]

(3) Actual evaporation from the store. \[E_s = \frac{S\left(2-\frac{S}{x_1}\right)\tanh\!\frac{E_n}{x_1}} {1+\left(1-\frac{S}{x_1}\right)\tanh\!\frac{E_n}{x_1}} \tag{9}\]

(4) Store update. \(\;S \leftarrow S - E_s + P_s\).

(5) Percolation. \[\mathrm{Perc} = S\left[1-\left(1+\left(\tfrac{4}{9}\tfrac{S}{x_1}\right)^{4}\right)^{-1/4}\right], \qquad S \leftarrow S - \mathrm{Perc} \tag{10}\]

(6) Routed volume. \(\;P_r = \mathrm{Perc} + (P_n - P_s)\), split 90 % / 10 %.

(7) Unit hydrographs. With \(\mathrm{SH}_1(u)=0\) for \(u\le0\), \((u/x_4)^{5/2}\) for \(0<u<x_4\), \(1\) for \(u\ge x_4\); and \(\mathrm{SH}_2(u)= \tfrac12 (u/x_4)^{5/2}\) for \(0<u<x_4\), \(1-\tfrac12(2-u/x_4)^{5/2}\) for \(x_4\le u<2x_4\), \(1\) for \(u\ge 2x_4\): \[u^{(1)}_j = \mathrm{SH}_1(j)-\mathrm{SH}_1(j-1),\quad j=1..\lceil x_4\rceil;\qquad u^{(2)}_j = \mathrm{SH}_2(j)-\mathrm{SH}_2(j-1),\quad j=1..\lceil 2x_4\rceil \tag{11}\] \[Q_9 = 0.9\sum_j u^{(1)}_j P_r(t-j+1),\qquad Q_1 = 0.1\sum_j u^{(2)}_j P_r(t-j+1) \tag{12}\]

(8) Groundwater exchange. \(\;F = x_2\left(\dfrac{R}{x_3}\right)^{7/2}\)

(9) Routing store. \(\;R \leftarrow \max(0,\;R + Q_9 + F)\), then \[Q_r = R\left[1-\left(1+\left(\tfrac{R}{x_3}\right)^{4}\right)^{-1/4}\right],\qquad R \leftarrow R - Q_r \tag{13}\]

(10) Output. \(\;Q_d = \max(0,\,Q_1+F)\), \(\;\boxed{Q_t = Q_r + Q_d}\)

For small \(R/x_3\), Equation 13 expands to \(Q_r \approx R^5/(4x_3^4)\): a strongly non-linear storage–discharge relation, which is precisely why GR4J can reproduce a \(b>1\) recession with a single store.

7 Slot 3 — HBV-light without snow

7.1 Overview

HBV (Bergström 1992; Lindström et al. 1997) in the “HBV-light” formulation of Seibert and Vis (2012), with the snow routine removed (a01 decision: snow = 0.8 % of \(P\); DECISIONS.md). Nine parameters: three for the soil routine, five for the response routine, one for routing. HBV is the best-performing conceptual model documented at this gauge (calibrated NSE 0.819; uncalibrated 0.687 — a01, after Kratzert et al. (2019)).

Why for the Leaf River. It is the only structure in the portfolio with an explicit storage threshold in the fast response: the outlet \(K_0\) only operates when \(S_{UZ} > UZL\). a02’s event analysis (runoff coefficients spanning two orders of magnitude and correlated with antecedent wetness, \(r = 0.57\)) is exactly the signature of a threshold. It also carries the two subsurface time scales demanded by Section 2 (\(K_1 \approx 0.1\) d⁻¹ ⇒ \(\tau \approx 10\) d; \(K_2 \approx 0.002\) d⁻¹ ⇒ \(\tau \approx 500\) d), and its \(\beta\)-function recharge is a different parameterisation of the variable contributing area than GR4J’s \(\tanh\) production store — good for ensemble diversity.

7.4 Mathematical formulation

States: \(SM\) (mm, \(0\le SM\le FC\)), \(S_{UZ}\) (mm), \(S_{LZ}\) (mm), and the MAXBAS memory. Order of operations within day \(t\) (explicit Euler / operator splitting):

(1) Recharge (soil routine). \[R_t = P_t\left(\frac{SM_{t-1}}{FC}\right)^{\beta},\qquad SM^{*} = SM_{t-1} + P_t - R_t \tag{15}\] If \(SM^{*} > FC\), move the excess to recharge: \(R_t \leftarrow R_t + (SM^{*}-FC)\), \(SM^{*} \leftarrow FC\).

(2) Actual evaporation. \[E_{a,t} = \mathrm{PET}_t \cdot \min\!\left(\frac{SM^{*}}{LP\cdot FC},\,1\right),\qquad SM_t = \max\!\big(SM^{*} - E_{a,t},\,0\big) \tag{16}\] (if the \(\max\) clips, reduce \(E_{a,t}\) by the clipped amount so mass is conserved).

(3) Percolation. \[\mathrm{PERC}_t = \min\big(\mathrm{PERC},\; S_{UZ,t-1} + R_t\big),\qquad S^{*}_{UZ} = S_{UZ,t-1} + R_t - \mathrm{PERC}_t,\qquad S^{*}_{LZ} = S_{LZ,t-1} + \mathrm{PERC}_t \tag{17}\]

(4) Response routine (with the effective constants \(K_0^{\text{eff}}=\max(K_0,K_1)\) and \(K_2^{\text{eff}}=\min(K_2,\,0.9K_1)\), see Section 7.5): \[Q_{0,t}=K_0^{\text{eff}}\max\!\big(S^{*}_{UZ}-UZL,\,0\big),\qquad Q_{1,t}=K_1 S^{*}_{UZ},\qquad Q_{2,t}=K_2^{\text{eff}} S^{*}_{LZ} \tag{18}\] \[S_{UZ,t} = S^{*}_{UZ}-Q_{0,t}-Q_{1,t},\qquad S_{LZ,t}=S^{*}_{LZ}-Q_{2,t} \tag{19}\] If \(Q_{0,t}+Q_{1,t} > S^{*}_{UZ}\) (possible only for extreme \(K\) combinations), scale both down by \(S^{*}_{UZ}/(Q_{0,t}+Q_{1,t})\) and set \(S_{UZ,t}=0\).

(5) Routing. Generated runoff \(Q^{\text{gen}}_t = Q_{0,t}+Q_{1,t}+Q_{2,t}\) is convolved with the triangular weights obtained by integrating the unit-area isosceles triangle \(h(u)\) on \([0,\mathrm{MAXBAS}]\): \[w_j=\int_{j-1}^{j} h(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,\quad j=1..\lceil \mathrm{MAXBAS}\rceil,\qquad h(u)=\frac{4}{\mathrm{MAXBAS}^2}\min\!\big(u,\ \mathrm{MAXBAS}-u\big)_+ \tag{20}\] \[w_j \leftarrow w_j\Big/\sum_k w_k,\qquad \boxed{\,Q_t=\sum_j w_j\,Q^{\text{gen}}_{t-j+1}\,} \tag{21}\]

The optimizer therefore searches a box; the effective values must be reported by a13 alongside the raw ones.

  2. Baseflow. \(B_p = LZFPC\,D_{LZP}\), \(LZFPC \mathrel{-}= B_p\); \(B_s = LZFSC\,D_{LZS}\), \(LZFSC \mathrel{-}= B_s\); accumulate \(\mathrm{SBF} \mathrel{+}= B_p+B_s\).
  3. Percolation (only if \(P_{\text{inc}}+UZFWC>2\cdot10^{-6}\)): \[\mathrm{PERCM}=LZFPM\,D_{LZP}+LZFSM\,D_{LZS},\qquad \mathrm{DEFR}=1-\frac{LZTWC+LZFPC+LZFSC}{LZTWM+LZFPM+LZFSM}\] \[\mathrm{PERC}=\mathrm{PERCM}\Big(1+\mathrm{ZPERC}\,\mathrm{DEFR}^{\mathrm{REXP}}\Big)\frac{UZFWC}{UZFWM} \tag{27}\] capped by \(\mathrm{PERC}\le UZFWC\) and by the remaining remaining lower-zone capacity; then \(UZFWC \mathrel{-}= \mathrm{PERC}\).
  4. Interflow. \(\;\mathrm{DEL}=UZFWC\,D_{UZ}\), \(\;\mathrm{SIF}\mathrel{+}=\mathrm{DEL}\), \(\;UZFWC\mathrel{-}=\mathrm{DEL}\).
  5. Distribute percolation. \(\mathrm{PERCT}=\mathrm{PERC}(1-\mathrm{PFREE})\) goes to \(LZTWC\) (overflow \(\mathrm{PERCF}\) carries on); \(\mathrm{PERCF}\mathrel{+}=\mathrm{PERC}\cdot\mathrm{PFREE}\). Split \(\mathrm{PERCF}\) between the two free-water stores with \[\mathrm{HPL}=\frac{LZFPM}{LZFPM+LZFSM},\quad \mathrm{FRACP}=\min\!\left(1,\ \mathrm{HPL}\,\frac{2(1-LZFPC/LZFPM)}{(1-LZFPC/LZFPM)+(1-LZFSC/LZFSM)}\right) \tag{28}\] \(\mathrm{PERCP}=\mathrm{FRACP}\cdot\mathrm{PERCF}\) → \(LZFPC\), remainder → \(LZFSC\); any overflow of either store is returned to \(LZTWC\).
  6. Surface runoff. If \(P_{\text{inc}}+UZFWC>UZFWM\): \(\mathrm{SUR}=P_{\text{inc}}+UZFWC-UZFWM\), \(UZFWC=UZFWM\), \(\mathrm{SSUR}\mathrel{+}=\mathrm{SUR}\cdot \mathrm{PAREA}\), \(\mathrm{ADSUR}=\mathrm{SUR}\left(1-\mathrm{ADDRO}/P_{\text{inc}}\right)\), \(\mathrm{SSUR}\mathrel{+}=\mathrm{ADSUR}\cdot\mathrm{ADIMP}\); else \(UZFWC\mathrel{+}=P_{\text{inc}}\), \(\mathrm{ADSUR}=0\).
  7. ADIMC update. \(ADIMC \mathrel{+}= P_{\text{inc}}-\mathrm{ADDRO}-\mathrm{ADSUR}\); if \(ADIMC>UZTWM+LZTWM\), move the excess to \(\mathrm{ADDRO}\) and cap. Accumulate \(\mathrm{SDRO}\mathrel{+}=\mathrm{ADDRO}\cdot\mathrm{ADIMP}\).

(4.5) Channel inflow. \[\mathrm{TCI}_t = \mathrm{ROIMP} + \mathrm{SDRO} + \mathrm{SSUR} + \mathrm{SIF}\cdot \mathrm{PAREA} + \frac{\mathrm{SBF}\cdot \mathrm{PAREA}}{1+\mathrm{SIDE}} \tag{29}\] with \(\mathrm{SIDE}=0\) here. Total ET: \(\mathrm{TET}_t=(E_1+E_2+E_3)\mathrm{PAREA}+E_5\) (plus \(E_4=0\) since \(\mathrm{RIVA}=0\)).

(4.6) Routing — 2-parameter gamma (Nash) unit hydrograph (Nash 1957): \[u_j = F_\Gamma\!\big(j;\,\mathrm{UH\_N},\mathrm{UH\_K}\big)-F_\Gamma\!\big(j-1;\,\mathrm{UH\_N},\mathrm{UH\_K}\big), \qquad j = 1..n_{uh},\qquad u_j \leftarrow u_j\Big/\textstyle\sum_k u_k \tag{30}\] \[\boxed{\,Q_t=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{uh}} u_j\,\mathrm{TCI}_{t-j+1}\,},\qquad n_{uh}=\min\!\big(60,\ \min\{n: F_\Gamma(n)\ge0.9999\}\big)\] Mean lag \(=\mathrm{UH\_N}\cdot\mathrm{UH\_K}\) days, which the bounds place at 0.1–15 d with a prior of 1–3 d (a01).

8.8 Numerical pitfalls

  • Never skip the sub-daily incrementation (Equation 26). With a 100 mm/d storm (the record maximum is 221.5 mm/d, a02) a single explicit step drains the upper zone by more than its content.
  • Convert depletion rates with \(1-(1-K)^{\Delta}\), not \(K\Delta\).
  • Guard every store: if abs(x) < 1e-10: x = 0, and return the clipped amount to the flux that caused it.
  • Assert \(UZTWC \le ADIMC \le UZTWM+LZTWM\) every step; violating it is the classic SAC-SMA coding bug.
  • In step (f) \(\mathrm{ADDRO}/P_{\text{inc}}\) divides by zero when \(P_{\text{inc}}=0\); that branch is only reached when \(P_{\text{inc}}>0\), but code the guard explicitly.
  • \(\mathrm{DEFR}\) can be slightly negative from round-off → max(DEFR, 0) before the power in Equation 27 (\(\mathrm{REXP}\) may be fractional).
  • Equation 28 divides by \((1-LZFPC/LZFPM)+(1-LZFSC/LZFSM)\), which is 0 when both stores are full; guard with \(\max(\cdot,10^{-9})\) and clamp \(\mathrm{FRACP}\in[0,1]\).
  • numba.njit the daily loop; a pure-Python loop is ~100× too slow for \(10^4\)–\(10^5\) optimizer runs (Section 11).

9 Slot 5 — single-basin sequence-to-sequence LSTM

9.1 Overview

A Long Short-Term Memory network (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997) applied to rainfall–runoff modelling following Kratzert et al. (2018) and Kratzert et al. (2019). The LSTM’s cell state acts as a learned, multi-dimensional storage system whose “recharge” and “release” are gated by the forcing — conceptually the same role as the stores in slots 2–4, but with the partitioning learned rather than prescribed. Kratzert et al. (2019) report NSE 0.84–0.86 for a regional LSTM at this exact gauge; here we have only one basin, so the design is heavily regularised.

Why for the Leaf River. (i) It is the only family in the portfolio whose errors are not generated by a prescribed store-and-release structure, which is what makes the ensembles in Section 12 worth testing. (ii) a02’s memory analysis (linear \(R^2\) still rising at a 365-day lag) says the basin has exactly the kind of long, non-linear memory an LSTM is built for. (iii) 36 calibration water years is at the lower edge of single-basin feasibility, which is why dropout, weight decay, early stopping and 5-seed averaging are all prescribed rather than optional.

9.4 Mathematical formulation

Inputs. Base features (always): \[\mathbf{x}_t = \Big[\tfrac{P_t-3.9078}{10.1414},\;\tfrac{\mathrm{PET}_t-2.9004}{1.8890},\; \sin\tfrac{2\pi d_t}{365.25},\;\cos\tfrac{2\pi d_t}{365.25}\Big] \tag{32}\] If use_antecedent_features = 1, ten more columns are appended: \(A^{(\lambda)}_t=\lambda A^{(\lambda)}_{t-1}+P_t\) for \(\lambda\in\{0.98,0.93,0.80\}\); \(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}P_{t-i}\) for \(k\in\{7,30,90,365\}\); and \(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(P_{t-i}-\mathrm{PET}_{t-i})\) for \(k\in\{30,90,365\}\). Each of these ten is standardised with its own mean/sd computed on the calibration metric window only and frozen in the model artefact.

Cell. Standard LSTM equations, with \(\sigma\) the logistic function and \(\odot\) the Hadamard product: \[\mathbf{i}_t=\sigma(W_i\mathbf{x}_t+U_i\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\mathbf{b}_i),\qquad \mathbf{f}_t=\sigma(W_f\mathbf{x}_t+U_f\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\mathbf{b}_f) \tag{33}\] \[\mathbf{g}_t=\tanh(W_g\mathbf{x}_t+U_g\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\mathbf{b}_g),\qquad \mathbf{o}_t=\sigma(W_o\mathbf{x}_t+U_o\mathbf{h}_{t-1}+\mathbf{b}_o) \tag{34}\] \[\mathbf{c}_t=\mathbf{f}_t\odot\mathbf{c}_{t-1}+\mathbf{i}_t\odot\mathbf{g}_t,\qquad \mathbf{h}_t=\mathbf{o}_t\odot\tanh(\mathbf{c}_t) \tag{35}\]

Head and output transform (\(\mu_Q=1.3533\), \(\sigma_Q=2.8511\) mm/d from the calibration window): \[\hat y_t=\mathbf{w}^\top \mathrm{Dropout}(\mathbf{h}_t)+b,\qquad \boxed{\;\hat Q_t=\mathrm{softplus}\!\big(\mu_Q+\sigma_Q\,\hat y_t\big)=\ln\!\big(1+e^{\mu_Q+\sigma_Q\hat y_t}\big)\;} \tag{36}\] The softplus guarantees \(\hat Q_t>0\) (the river is perennial, a02) and keeps the gradient alive for the log-space loss term, which a hard clip would not.

Loss (\(\sigma_{\log Q}=1.2192\)): \[\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\frac{\hat Q_t-Q_t}{\sigma_Q}\right)^{2} \;+\;w_{\log}\,\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}} \left(\frac{\ln(\hat Q_t+10^{-6})-\ln Q_t}{\sigma_{\log Q}}\right)^{2} \tag{37}\] The first term is the NSE-equivalent objective on the reported metric; the second (weight \(w_{\log}\), default 0.25) protects the 40 % of days below 0.45 mm/d that the first term ignores (a02: the wettest 5 % of days carry 40 % of the volume). \(\mathcal{T}\) excludes the first warmup_steps of every training window.

Sequence handling. Training draws random contiguous windows of length seq_len (default 365 d) from the internal-train mask; the loss is evaluated only on steps \(t>\) warmup_steps (default 60), so the network learns to be accurate given as little as 60 days of context. At inference the model performs a single continuous forward pass over whatever frame simulate() receives, with \(\mathbf{h}_0=\mathbf{c}_0=\mathbf{0}\), emitting one \(\hat Q_t\) per day. This is what makes the LSTM comparable to the conceptual slots in the evaluation window, where only 92 warm-up days exist before the metric period begins (a01/a16 note).

9.8 Numerical pitfalls

  • Leakage: training windows must be drawn from internal_mask(frame, "train") only, and the standardisation constants must come from config/models.yaml → scaling: — never recomputed on the frame given at prediction time. load() must restore them; a silent re-standardisation on the evaluation frame would invalidate the whole study.
  • Use torch.nn.functional.softplus (numerically stable for large arguments), and \(\ln(\hat Q+10^{-6})\) in Equation 37.
  • MPS is not bit-reproducible for all kernels; set torch.manual_seed(seed) per member, and if the 5-member mean is not reproducible across runs, fall back to cpu and record it in the a09/a15 handoff (CLAUDE.md rule 7). Device via src.common.data.torch_device().
  • Round hidden_size, n_layers, seq_len, warmup_steps, batch_size, use_antecedent_features to integers when they arrive from a continuous optimizer.
  • The rolling 365-day features need 365 leading days; inside the evaluation frame those do not exist — compute them with a growing (partial) window and divide by the number of available days, i.e. pandas.Series.rolling(k, min_periods=1).mean() * k.     """Non-authoritative timing prototype (a06 writes the deliverable). Perrin et al. (2003)."""
    n = P.shape[0]
    nu1, nu2 = u1.shape[0], u2.shape[0]
    S, R = 0.30 * x1, 0.50 * x3
    m1 = np.zeros(nu1); m2 = np.zeros(nu2)
    Q = np.empty(n)
    for t in range(n):
        p = P[t]; e = E[t]
        if p >= e:
            pn = p - e; en = 0.0
        else:
            pn = 0.0; en = e - p
        s = S / x1
        ps = 0.0; es = 0.0
        if pn > 0.0:
            th = np.tanh(min(pn / x1, 13.0))
            ps = x1 * (1.0 - s * s) * th / (1.0 + s * th)
        else:
            th = np.tanh(min(en / x1, 13.0))
            es = S * (2.0 - s) * th / (1.0 + (1.0 - s) * th)
        S = S - es + ps
        perc = S * (1.0 - (1.0 + (4.0 / 9.0 * S / x1) ** 4) ** -0.25)
        S -= perc
        pr = perc + (pn - ps)
        q9 = m1[0] + 0.9 * pr * u1[0]
        for k in range(nu1 - 1):
            m1[k] = m1[k + 1] + 0.9 * pr * u1[k + 1]
        m1[nu1 - 1] = 0.0
        q1 = m2[0] + 0.1 * pr * u2[0]
        for k in range(nu2 - 1):
            m2[k] = m2[k + 1] + 0.1 * pr * u2[k + 1]
        m2[nu2 - 1] = 0.0
        f = x2 * (R / x3) ** 3.5
        R = R + q9 + f
        if R < 0.0:
            R = 0.0
        qr = R * (1.0 - (1.0 + (R / x3) ** 4) ** -0.25)
        R -= qr

14.2 Why this is not leakage

An out-of-band result of this kind is usually a leak, so it was checked structurally rather than argued away. Four things were verified in src/models/model_1.py:

  1. Observed discharge never enters the design matrix. The regressors are precipitation, PET and exponentially-weighted antecedent-precipitation indices. The “AR” in ARX is autoregression in the API state, not on observed \(Q\) — so the model remains comparable with the conceptual slots, which is exactly what config/project.yaml -> ml_state_note requires.
  2. The multiplicative bias factor \(c\) is frozen at fit time. It is computed once inside fit() from the fitting window and only read in simulate(). It is not recomputed from the window being predicted — which would have been a textbook leak, and would have manufactured \(\beta \approx 1\) on any period.
  3. Column means and standard deviations are likewise frozen in _fitted and restored by load().
  4. The lazy-fit fallback routes to the calibration window through the guarded loader, never to the window passed to simulate(). The leak-safe path is the default, not an option.

The evaluation window played no part in any of this: src/common/data.py raises PermissionError unless LR_AGENT=a16, and no test or model sets that variable.

14.3 What it does and does not imply

It does not yet falsify H1. Two reasons to withhold judgement until a16:

  • These are calibration-period and internal-validation numbers. H1 is stated on the evaluation metric window 1949-01-01…1951-09-30, which no agent except a16 may touch.
  • That window is the wettest stretch of the record — mean \(Q\) ratio 1.29 versus calibration, runoff ratio 0.41 against 0.35 (a02). A regression carries no mass balance and no storage ceiling, so it has more room to degrade under a wetness shift than a conceptual model does. The conceptual slots also arrive at a16 calibrated, whereas the numbers above are mostly defaults.

What it does imply is that the interesting question has moved. The study was designed to measure how much complexity buys; it may instead measure how little. If slot 1 holds its ground on the evaluation window, the honest headline is that on a humid, energy-limited, snow-free basin with 40 years of clean forcing, a well-posed linear model in transformed space is competitive with conceptual hydrology — and H1, as pre-registered, is falsified in its first clause.

a16 must report that outcome plainly if it occurs, including the possibility that NSE(slot 1) > NSE(slot 4). A pre-registered hypothesis that fails is a result, not an error.

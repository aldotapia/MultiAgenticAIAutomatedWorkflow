ImportantRecommendation in one box

Calibrate every model slot (a11–a15) by MAXIMISING the equally weighted average of the two metrics that will be reported:

\[ \mathcal{O} \;=\; 0.5\,\mathrm{KGE}\!\left(Q_{\text{sim}},Q_{\text{obs}}\right) \;+\; 0.5\,\mathrm{NSE}\!\left(Q_{\text{sim}},Q_{\text{obs}}\right) \]

on untransformed daily flow (mm/day), KGE as defined by Gupta et al. (2009) and NSE by Nash and Sutcliffe (1970) (the project’s canonical src/common/metrics.py), evaluated on the calibration metric window 1952-10-01 … 1988-09-30 (13 149 days), warm-up 1951-10-01 … 1952-09-30 excluded. Optimisers that minimise use the cost \(1-\mathcal{O}\) (perfect = 0, unbounded above). Implementation: src.common.metrics_extra.objective / objective_cost; contract: config/metrics.yaml.

No transform and therefore no offset \(\varepsilon\) anywhere. Observed \(Q\ge0.069\) mm/day on all 14 610 days, so \(\sqrt{\cdot}\), \(\log\) and Box–Cox would be defined without an arbitrary constant (Section 3) — the point is that they are not needed: a third \(\mathrm{KGE}(\sqrt{Q})\) term was tested explicitly and changed every out-of-sample number by less than 0.001.

Why not plain KGE, the ranking metric? Because Section 6 calibrates two surrogate rainfall–runoff models under 24 candidate objectives on four independent splits inside the calibration period (three of them 20–46 % wetter than their training years, mimicking the evaluation window, which is 29 % wetter than calibration). Result, as mean shortfall behind the best objective of each of the 8 experiments (“regret”):

Trainable models (a09, LSTM) minimise the identical quantity as a full-batch loss (population standard deviations); a per-mini-batch KGE is a different objective.

Reported metrics are fixed and are NOT chosen here: a16 reports KGE and NSE on untransformed \(Q\) only, ranked by KGE, per config/project.yaml.

1 Scope and constraints

The metrics in the final report are fixed by the user and by config/project.yaml (evaluation_protocol.required_metrics: [KGE, NSE], primary_selection_metric: KGE, final_report_metrics_only: true). Agent 4 therefore does not choose the reporting metrics. What Agent 4 chooses is the calibration objective: the scalar that a10’s optimisers and a11–a15’s calibration runs drive to its optimum. Objective \(\ne\) reporting metric is a deliberate and well-documented distinction (Clark et al. 2021; Mizukami et al. 2019): the objective is an estimator design choice whose only job is to put parameters where the reported metrics will be good on data the calibration never saw.

\[ \mathrm{MSE}=\frac1n\sum (s_i-o_i)^2,\qquad \mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\mathrm{MSE}},\qquad \mathrm{NSE}=1-\frac{\sum (s_i-o_i)^2}{\sum (o_i-\mu_o)^2} \]

NoteInformation-theoretic and probabilistic scores

Mutual information \(I(s;o)\), CRPS and Wasserstein distances were considered. CRPS requires a predictive distribution; all five model slots are deterministic and the final ranking is deterministic, so CRPS is only meaningful if an ensemble is submitted — it is therefore not part of the objective and not part of the report. The 1-Wasserstein distance between FDCs is implemented (FDC_W1_log) because it is a pure distribution-shape diagnostic: a model can have \(W_1=0\) and \(r=0\), which makes it a clean complement to KGE’s \(r\) term. Mutual information was rejected as an objective: on 13 149 daily values it needs binning/KDE choices that are themselves tuning parameters, and it is invariant to any monotone transform — including a 50 % volume bias.

Fact 1 — one per cent of the days own the squared-error criterion. In the calibration metric window the wettest 1 % of days (131 of 13 149) carry 15.7 % of the volume but 58.3 % of \(\sum(o-\mu_o)^2\); the wettest 5 % carry 83.6 %. Any SSE-based objective (MSE, RMSE, NSE, Gaussian likelihood — all with the same argmax) is therefore, in effect, fitted to ~130 flood days. The \(\sqrt{\cdot}\) transform reduces the 1 % share to 26.8 % and \(\log\) to 8.7 %: these numbers, not a preference, are what “balancing high and low flows” means here.

Fact 2 — NSE is nearly blind to volume bias at this CV, KGE is not. For a simulation with the right shape but a volume ratio \(\beta=c\) (\(r=1\), \(\alpha=c\)),

\[ \mathrm{KGE}=1-\sqrt2\,|c-1| \quad\text{(both } \alpha \text{ and } \beta \text{ terms)},\qquad \mathrm{NSE}=1-\Big(\tfrac{c-1}{\mathrm{CV}_o}\Big)^2\ \text{if only the mean is wrong}, \]

and \(\mathrm{CV}_o=2.107\) here.

A model that is 10 % too wet loses 0.100 KGE but only 0.0023 NSE — a factor 44. This is the single most important asymmetry for this project: calibrating on NSE lets the optimiser trade water balance for a slightly better fit to floods almost for free, and that debt is paid in full in the KGE ranking — and it is paid on a period 29 % wetter than the one the trade was made on. The same algebra explains why KGE″ (Tang et al. 2021), whose bias term is \(\beta_n=(\mu_s-\mu_o)/\sigma_o\), behaves almost like NSE here: at CV = 2.107 it divides the bias by 2.1 before squaring it.

Fact 3 — no offset \(\varepsilon\) is needed. a02 verified \(Q>0\) on all 14 610 days (min 0.069 mm/day, quantised at 1 ft³/s over 1 949 km²). Every \(\log\), \(\sqrt{}\), \(1/Q\) and Box–Cox statistic in this report is therefore computed with \(\varepsilon=0\), which removes the usual arbitrary-constant problem (Pushpalatha et al. 2012; Santos et al. 2018): an \(\varepsilon\) is a hidden tuning parameter that silently sets which low flows the objective can see. Only simulated flows are floored (at \(10^{-12}\) mm/day) so that a degenerate model cannot return \(-\infty\).

What the fingerprint says, in the order that matters for the decision:

  1. Volume bias. ±20 % volume costs KGE 0.283 but NSE only 0.049 — the algebra of Table 7 confirmed empirically. log-NSE (0.97) and sqrt-NSE (0.97) are even blinder. Only the KGE family, PBIAS/VE and FHV react properly.
  2. Timing. A one-day shift costs NSE 0.220 but KGE only 0.110 (KGE loses only through \(r\)). NSE is the stronger timing criterion; KGE is the stronger water-balance criterion. They are complementary, which is exactly why the project reports both.
  3. Variance errors. Inflating the variance in log space by 30 % gives KGE −1.17 and NSE −3.27 while log-NSE stays at 0.910 and KGE_log at 0.700. A log-based objective literally cannot see a catastrophic error in the raw-flow variance — a decisive argument against calibrating on \(\log Q\) when the report is on raw \(Q\) (Santos et al. 2018).
  4. Low-flow instability. Adding 0.3 mm/d of white noise (a trivial error at flood flows) leaves KGE at 0.976 but drives log-NSE to −1.50 and inverse-NSE to −15 554. Inverse and log objectives are numerically dominated by the smallest flows of a perennial river; they are unusable as a primary objective here and are kept only as diagnostics.
  5. Over-smoothing (7-day moving average, i.e. a model with too much storage) is punished by KGE (0.785 via \(\alpha\) = 0.81) and by FHV (−20 %) but barely by log-NSE (0.913) — another transform-blindness.
  6. Peak truncation (top 2 % cut by 30 %) costs KGE 0.229 and NSE 0.074 but improves log-NSE and inverse-NSE to ≈1.00. FHV = −27.6 % detects it cleanly, which is why FHV is retained as the mandatory high-flow diagnostic.

6.1 Design

  • Surrogate 1 — HyMOD (5 parameters: \(C_{max}\), \(b_{exp}\), \(\alpha\), \(R_s\), \(R_q\)): a PDM storage (Moore 1985) feeding one slow and three quick linear reservoirs. HyMOD is the classic Leaf River model (Boyle et al. 2000; Vrugt et al. 2003), which makes it the most defensible surrogate available.
  • Surrogate 2 — NLRes (5 parameters: \(S_{max}\), \(\beta\), \(d\), \(k_f\), \(k_s\)): HBV-style power-law saturation runoff, a linear fast reservoir and a non-linear slow reservoir \(q=k_sS^{1.5}\), chosen because a02 found a non-linear recession (Brutsaert–Nieber \(b=1.68\)). Two structurally different surrogates guard against a conclusion that is an artefact of one model.
  • Optimiser: SciPy differential_evolution, seed 42, popsize 15, maxiter 150, tol \(10^{-8}\), polish — identical budget for every objective, so differences are attributable to the objective.

from numba import njit, prange

@njit(cache=True, fastmath=True)
def _hymod(P, E, cmax, bexp, alpha, Rs, Rq):
    n = P.shape[0]; Q = np.empty(n)
    x_loss = 0.0; x_slow = 2.3503 / (Rs * 22.5); x_q1 = x_q2 = x_q3 = 0.0
    b1 = bexp + 1.0; cm_b = cmax / b1
    for t in range(n):
        Pval = P[t]; PETval = E[t]; xn_prev = x_loss
        arg = 1.0 - b1 * xn_prev / cmax
        if arg < 0.0: arg = 0.0
        ct_prev = cmax * (1.0 - arg ** (1.0 / b1))
        UT1 = Pval - cmax + ct_prev
        if UT1 < 0.0: UT1 = 0.0
        Pval = Pval - UT1
        dummy = (ct_prev + Pval) / cmax
        if dummy > 1.0: dummy = 1.0
        xn = cm_b * (1.0 - (1.0 - dummy) ** b1)
        UT2 = Pval - (xn - xn_prev)
        if UT2 < 0.0: UT2 = 0.0
        evap = (1.0 - (cm_b - xn) / cm_b) * PETval
        if evap > xn: evap = xn
        x_loss = xn - evap
        UQ = alpha * (UT1 + UT2); US = (1.0 - alpha) * (UT1 + UT2)
        x_slow = (1.0 - Rs) * x_slow + (1.0 - Rs) * US; QS = (Rs / (1.0 - Rs)) * x_slow
        x_q1 = (1.0 - Rq) * x_q1 + (1.0 - Rq) * UQ;  o1 = (Rq / (1.0 - Rq)) * x_q1
        x_q2 = (1.0 - Rq) * x_q2 + (1.0 - Rq) * o1;  o2 = (Rq / (1.0 - Rq)) * x_q2
        x_q3 = (1.0 - Rq) * x_q3 + (1.0 - Rq) * o2;  o3 = (Rq / (1.0 - Rq)) * x_q3
        q = QS + o3
        Q[t] = q if q > 1e-9 else 1e-9
    return Q

@njit(cache=True, fastmath=True)
def _nlres(P, E, Smax, beta, d, kf, ks):
    n = P.shape[0]; Q = np.empty(n)
    S = 0.5 * Smax; Sf = 0.0; Ss = 10.0
    for t in range(n):
        p = P[t]; ratio = S / Smax
        if ratio > 1.0: ratio = 1.0
        r = p * ratio ** beta
        S = S + p - r
        ea = E[t] * (S / Smax)
        if ea > S: ea = S
        S = S - ea
        if S > Smax:
            r += S - Smax; S = Smax
        Sf = Sf + d * r; qf = kf * Sf; Sf = Sf - qf
        Ss = Ss + (1.0 - d) * r; qs = ks * Ss ** 1.5
        if qs > Ss: qs = Ss
        Ss = Ss - qs
        q = qf + qs
        Q[t] = q if q > 1e-9 else 1e-9
    return Q

@njit(parallel=True, cache=True)
def simulate_batch(P, E, theta, which):
    ns = theta.shape[0]; nt = P.shape[0]; out = np.empty((ns, nt))
    for i in prange(ns):
        if which == 0:
            out[i] = _hymod(P, E, theta[i, 0], theta[i, 1], theta[i, 2], theta[i, 3], theta[i, 4])
        else:
            out[i] = _nlres(P, E, theta[i, 0], theta[i, 1], theta[i, 2], theta[i, 3], theta[i, 4])
    return out

    r = np.random.default_rng(seed); d = bounds.shape[0]; u = np.empty((n, d))

    return bounds[:, 0] + u * (bounds[:, 1] - bounds[:, 0])

7.1 The decision

Calibrate on \(\mathcal{O}=0.5\,\mathrm{KGE}(Q)+0.5\,\mathrm{NSE}(Q)\) — maximise — on 1952-10-01…1988-09-30; equivalently minimise \(1-\mathcal{O}\).

This is not the answer the catalogue alone would have suggested (a KGE + \(\sqrt{Q}\) composite); it is what Section 6 produced. The decisive numbers:

Criterion Evidence
Alignment with the reported metrics The objective is the unweighted average of the two reported metrics on untransformed \(Q\) — nothing is optimised that is not reported, and nothing reported is left out. No transform, no \(\varepsilon\), no tuning constant other than the 50/50 weight.
The two terms are not redundant Over 6 000 behavioural HyMOD simulations the Spearman rank correlation between KGE and NSE is only ≈0.7 (Figure 6): KGE and NSE genuinely disagree about which parameter set is better on this record, so averaging them is a real multi-criteria compromise, not a re-scaling.
Robustness to the skew and to volume bias KGE’s \(\beta\) term supplies the water-balance control that NSE lacks at CV = 2.107 (factor 14–89, Table 7), while NSE’s stronger timing sensitivity (Section 5 item 2) compensates KGE’s weaker one. The pair covers each other’s blind spots.
Transfer to a wetter period Table 12: worst-case regret 0.056 (KGE) and 0.047 (NSE) — the only candidate of the 24 that keeps both below 0.06. KGE-only calibration has a worst case of 0.162 in NSE, NSE-only 0.107 in KGE. On the wet blocks the recommendation reaches mean KGE 0.773 / NSE 0.725 against 0.783/0.697 (KGE-only) and 0.743/0.734 (NSE-only): it gives up ≈0.010 KGE to gain ≈0.028 NSE.
Parsimony Three further variants were run through all 8 experiments: \((\mathrm{KGE}+\mathrm{NSE}+\mathrm{KGE}(\sqrt Q))/3\), \(0.4/0.4/0.2\) and \(0.7\,\mathrm{KGE}+0.3\,\mathrm{NSE}\). The first two are indistinguishable from the recommendation (all out-of-sample means differ by < 0.001); the 70/30 weighting trades 0.004 KGE for 0.008 NSE and doubles the worst case. The simplest member of the tied group wins.
Optimiser and ML compatibility Smooth, bounded above by 1, ~0.9 ms per evaluation on 13 149 days, and differentiable everywhere — a10’s global searches, a11–a15’s calibrations and a09’s gradient training all use the same scalar.
NoteHonest limitation

The recommendation does not improve low flows: out-of-sample log-NSE on the wet blocks is 0.666 for the recommendation against 0.761 for sqrt-NSE and 0.591 for KGE-only, and the high-flow bias FHV is −16.9 % against −12.3 % for KGE-only. Balancing the hydrograph and maximising the two reported metrics are different goals, and config/project.yaml asks for the second. The low-flow and high-flow behaviour is therefore controlled through the acceptance checks in config/metrics.yaml (|PBIAS| ≤ 10 %, \(\beta\in[0.90,1.10]\), \(\alpha\in[0.85,1.15]\), \(|\mathrm{FHV}| \le 25\) %, \(|\Delta\mathrm{BFI}|\le0.15\)), which a11–a15 must report, not through the objective. A slot that passes the acceptance checks and maximises \(\mathcal{O}\) is both hydrologically sound and competitive in the ranking.

7.2 Why not each alternative

  • Plain KGE (2009). The ranking metric, and the declared fallback. It gives the best out-of-sample KGE (regret 0.020 vs 0.026) — but the worst NSE of the whole KGE family (regret 0.047, worst case 0.162, wet-block NSE 0.697). Since NSE is reported too, paying 0.006 KGE to remove that exposure is the better bet.
  • NSE. Nearly blind to volume bias at CV = 2.107 (Table 7): a 10 % volume error costs 0.0023 NSE and 0.100 KGE. NSE-only calibrations produced the largest \(|\beta-1|\) on every wet test block (mean \(\beta\) = 1.103 vs 1.089) and the largest KGE regret (0.052). Never alone.
  • RMSE / MSE / Gaussian likelihood. Monotone transformations of each other and of NSE for a fixed observation window — identical argmax, identical weakness. Not separate options. (LL_bc025 and NSE_bc025 returned bit-identical parameter sets in Table 10, which is the empirical demonstration of that equivalence.)
  • KGE′ (2012). \(\gamma=\mathrm{CV}_s/\mathrm{CV}_o\) cancels a pure scaling error, so a uniformly biased model is penalised once instead of twice. It wins single wet blocks (KGE 0.909 on WY1961–63) but has the worst NSE regret of the KGE family (0.118) and out-of-sample log-NSE 0.11: it buys KGE by wrecking the low-flow end.
  • KGE″ (2021). Built for \(\mu_o\to0\) (ephemeral rivers). At CV = 2.107 it divides the bias by 2.1 before squaring and degenerates towards NSE; on the temporal split it was strictly worse than KGE for both surrogates. Leaf River is perennial — there is nothing to fix.
  • Non-parametric KGE. Robust and FDC-based, and the best objective for HyMOD on the temporal split, but it collapsed for the second surrogate (test NSE 0.257) because it optimises ranks and FDC shape rather than event magnitudes. Mean KGE regret 0.077. Retained as a diagnostic.
  • log-NSE / log-KGE / inverse-NSE. Section 5 items 3–4: they cannot see a 30 % raw-variance error and they explode on 0.3 mm/d of low-flow noise. In the transfer experiment KGE_log was the worst objective tested (mean KGE regret 0.181, NSE regret 0.579; test NSE −0.47 for one surrogate) and inverse-NSE was second worst. With only 92 warm-up days before the evaluation metric window, an objective dominated by the slowest store is also the most exposed to initial-state error.
  • MAE. A genuinely strong performer (best mean test KGE on the temporal split) and robust to the tail, but it is not one of the reported metrics, it has no diagnostic decomposition, and its worst-case KGE regret (0.091) is 1.6× the recommendation’s.
  • Formal likelihoods (GL, AR(1)). Only justified if a10 chooses DREAM and the deliverable were predictive uncertainty. They add 3–5 nuisance parameters and, in the Gaussian case, reduce to NSE.
  • Signature / Pareto multi-objective. A Pareto front cannot be handed to a11–a15 as a single number and the final ranking is single-objective by construction. Signatures are used as acceptance checks instead (Section 8).
